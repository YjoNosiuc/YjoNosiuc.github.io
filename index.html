kmsar / research

Faculty: research submissions with workflow status

Solo, full-stackPilot testedPlaywright

KMSAR

Knowledge Management System for Academic Research, Angeles University Foundation

Problem
Faculty research records for the research office (OVPRI), CDAIC, and the colleges were scattered, and approvals were tracked manually.
What it does
Research registration, a multi-stage approval flow through the dean and OVPRI, document uploads, dashboards per role, and bulk import of faculty accounts.
Tech
LaravelPHPMySQLBladeChart.jsPlaywright
Read case study

My role

I built the system on my own, from database design to deployment preparation, and worked with the research office to match their real process.

Highlights

  • Separate dashboards for super admin, OVPRI and CDAIC admins, deans and unit heads, and faculty.
  • A two-cycle workflow: research is registered first, then reviewed again when it is completed, with endorse or return at each step.
  • Reports by college, progress stage and Sustainable Development Goal, plus audit logs of important actions.
  • Email OTP password reset and an idle-session timeout for shared office computers.

What I learned

Designing a workflow is mostly about edge cases: returned submissions, co-authors from other colleges, and records that existed before the system. Pilot testing with deans and OVPRI staff showed me what to fix first.

GANAP PH

Event registration system for barangays

Problem
Barangay events are usually announced on paper or social media, with no easy way for residents to sign up or for officials to control slots.
What it does
Residents browse barangay events and register online. Remaining slots update in real time, paid events go through online checkout, each registration gets a QR code for check-in, and automations handle follow-up tasks.
Tech
LaravelVueInertia.jsLaravel EchoStripeMake.comPlaywright
Read case study

My role

Full-stack developer on the team, and I also handled the integrations: Stripe payments, real-time updates and Make.com automations.

Highlights

  • Four roles: super admin across barangays, barangay admin, staff for QR check-in, and residents.

  • End-to-end tests with Playwright for the main flows.

yc-connect / leads

Leads: tripping requests to confirm, interest levels and budgets

AI, final projectSolo, full-stackPDC60

YC Connect

Messenger sales desk for Yoj Cuison Real Estate, with an AI bot named David

Problem
Property inquiries on my Facebook page arrive at all hours, and buyers need correct prices and terms fast. One wrong price costs trust.
What it does
David answers buyers on Messenger using only facts I've approved, collects leads and site-tripping requests, and passes on what he can't answer. A phone-first admin is where I keep his knowledge correct and act on new leads.
Tech
Next.jsReactTypeScriptTailwindPostgreSQL + pgvectorDrizzleGemini · Grok · GroqMessenger API
Read case study

Highlights

  • An in-app Assistant reads my Google Drive folders (price lists, brochures, computations) and proposes changes as cards. Nothing reaches buyers until I confirm, and every change can be undone.
  • Projects are Draft or Live, and David only talks about Live projects.
  • Questions David can't answer from approved facts go to a Questions inbox instead of being guessed. Once I answer, he knows it from then on.
  • Leads get an interest level from what buyers actually said, and tripping requests land on a calendar for me to confirm.
  • If one AI provider is down or over quota, it falls back to another, and the admin shows daily AI usage.

Why it matters

It applies what I learned in PDC60: grounding, prompt-injection defense, data privacy under RA 10173, and keeping a human in the final decision. See the full write-up →

04 / Approach

How I work

The process I try to follow, and keep improving.

Understand the need

Talk to the people who will use it and write down what they actually need before building.

Build in small steps

Plan the database and screens, then build one feature at a time so problems are easy to trace.

Test as I go

Write automated tests for the important flows so changes don't break what already works.

Listen and improve

Let real users try it, collect their feedback, and fix what matters most first.
